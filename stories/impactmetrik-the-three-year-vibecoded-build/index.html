ImpactMetrik: A Three-Year, Vibecoded Build

From time to time we are inspired to build. Inspired to follow a path. Inspired to own something and be part of it. Inspired to create.

Why do I say all this? In December, I set out to build a Monitoring and Evaluation platform.

I have the experience and the knowledge. I've built things for people through jobs, gigs, projects — you name it. Never mine, though. Never something built for the masses, on my own terms, carrying my own name.

Starting without the map

I knew I wanted to build something. What I didn't do was consider the markets, or study the other tools already out there. Doing that would have meant giving up before I started — every established player would have looked like a reason not to bother.

Instead, I needed two things first: domain expertise, and at least one person who would buy into the dream. I had the first. Finding the second is its own story.

Then I needed a place to start. So I asked myself the obvious question — what is the most critical thing in M&E? The answer is reporting. And what feeds reporting? Collection.

That made the next step easy: we need a survey tool.

That's how surveys.impactmetrik.com was born — a tool built from the ground up, still in beta, that collects data and lets the user build insights from it. As simple as that.

Why this is a series, and why three years

This is the first post in a series about the build, and it's going to span three years. Not because the tool needs three years to write — because the idea does, if it's going to mean anything:

  1. We have to test with real clients.
  2. We get feedback and iterate.
  3. We validate the idea fully.

No shortcuts through any of those three. A tool nobody's actually used under real conditions isn't validated, it's just deployed.

And in the age of AI

This is going to be vibecoded from A to Z. That's not a disclaimer — it's the point. Building an M&E platform used to require a team, a runway, and months before you had anything a client could touch. That calculus has changed. I intend to document exactly how it changes it, mistakes included.

More posts in this series as surveys.impactmetrik.com moves from beta toward something real clients can lean on.
