## The race to the bottom

I've had this conversation too many times. Developer quotes a project. Client balks. Developer immediately drops the number. Client accepts. Developer spends three months resenting the project.

The problem isn't the client. The problem is that we anchor our pricing to what we _think_ the local market will bear, rather than what the work is actually worth. And because everyone does this, the anchor keeps dropping.

## The comparison that matters

A Laravel developer in Nairobi building a payment integration that processes KES 50M monthly is delivering the same value as one in Berlin doing the same thing. The code doesn't know where you live. The business impact doesn't discount for geography.

The client — especially if they're international — is not paying Nairobi rates. They're paying for the outcome. Price the outcome.

## What I changed

Three things shifted my pricing:

  * **I stopped quoting time.** I quote deliverables. "This integration" not "40 hours."
  * **I anchor to value, not cost.** If the feature will save the client 20 hours a month, that has a number. Start there.
  * **I raised my rates and lost some clients.** The ones who stayed were better. The work got more interesting. I stopped resenting my calendar.



> The clients who fight hardest on price are usually the ones who will cost you the most in other ways.
